Easy Crochet Mesh Beach Cover-Up Pattern

Pin this guide

A simple two-panel mesh cover-up using basic chain spaces and double crochet stitches, perfect for your first wearable summer project.

Updated 2026 · 8 min read

Making your first wearable item can feel intimidating, but a mesh beach cover-up is actually one of the easiest garments you will ever stitch. Because mesh fabric relies on large chain spaces and simple double crochets, it works up quickly without demanding intricate shaping or precise fitting. If you can chain and double crochet in straight lines, you already have all the core skills required to make this breezy summer layer.

In this guide, I will walk you through choosing lightweight yarn, taking basic body measurements, and constructing a seamless look using two simple rectangular panels. By keeping the structure straightforward, you get to build your confidence with garments while stitching a practical piece you will actually wear all summer.

Quick answer: To crochet a simple mesh beach cover-up, work two wide rectangular panels using alternating double crochets and chain spaces, then seam them at the shoulders and sides leaving openings for the head and arms. Cotton or bamboo blend DK weight yarn creates a soft drape that handles moisture well.

Choosing Yarn and Supplies for Beachwear

When making summer wear, yarn fiber choice matters far more than stitch complexity. Acrylic tends to trap heat and hold onto moisture, making it feel warm and soggy against damp swimwear. For a comfortable cover-up, choose a breathable sport or DK weight cotton, linen, or bamboo blend. Cotton provides great stitch definition and absorbs water quickly, while bamboo offers a soft, silky drape that hangs beautifully over your shoulders.

Pair your yarn with a hook size slightly larger than the label recommends — usually a 5.0 mm (H-8) or 5.5 mm (I-9) hook works best for mesh. The larger hook creates relaxed stitches and allows the mesh grid to open up naturally. You will also need a tapestry needle for seaming and two locking stitch markers to pin your sides in place before stitching.

  • Cotton or bamboo DK yarn (approx. 800-1100 yards)
  • 5.0 mm or 5.5 mm crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle and locking stitch markers

Mastering the Open Mesh Stitch

The mesh pattern relies on a fundamental chain-space repetition, often called the ladder stitch. You begin with an even number of foundation chains, plus three extra for your turning chain. In row one, you work a double crochet into the fifth chain from your hook, chain one, skip one chain, and work a double crochet into the next stitch. Repeating this pattern across creates a uniform grid of open squares.

On subsequent rows, chain four (which counts as your first double crochet plus a chain-one space), skip the first chain space, and work your double crochet directly into the double crochet stitch from the previous row. Working into the stitch tops rather than the open spaces creates clean, vertical columns that hold their shape over time without slumping, sagging, or stretching out of alignment.

Measuring and Sizing Your Panels

Garment sizing is blissfully easy for this pattern because you are stitching two identical rectangles. Measure the widest part of your upper body — usually the bust or hips — and add four to six inches for a relaxed, comfortable fit. Divide that total number by two to determine the width of each panel. For length, measure from the top of your shoulder down to your desired hemline.

Because mesh stitches stretch vertically when worn, keep in mind that yarn weight adds gravity. I recommend making your flat panels about two inches shorter than your target length, as the weight of the cotton will pull the hem down slightly once hung. Swatching a small 4x4 inch mesh square and suspending it overnight with a few clothespins gives you a precise preview of how your chosen yarn stretches.

Seaming and Finishing Touches

Lay your two finished panels flat with right sides facing together. Mark your shoulder seams with locking markers, leaving a wide neck opening in the center — usually eight to ten inches wide is comfortable. Use a tapestry needle and a mattress stitch or whipstitch to seam the shoulder tops together securely using your yarn tail.

Next, measure down eight to nine inches from the shoulder seams on both sides to leave armhole openings, and place markers. Seam down from those markers to the bottom edge, leaving a four-inch split at the side hem for easy movement. Fasten off, weave in all ends securely, and give your finished cover-up a light steam block to open up the mesh grid evenly.

Frequently asked questions

How many skeins of yarn do I need for a beach cover-up?

Most adult small to large cover-ups require between 800 and 1,100 yards of DK or sport weight cotton yarn, which usually equals 3 to 4 standard 100-gram skeins.

Can I use acrylic yarn for a summer cover-up?

While you can use acrylic, it is generally less breathable and holds heat against the skin, making lightweight cotton or linen blends much more comfortable for warm weather.

How do I prevent my mesh cover-up from stretching out?

Work your double crochets directly into the tops of stitches rather than into open chain spaces, and avoid hanging your finished wet cover-up on standard clothes hangers to dry.

Is this cover-up pattern suitable for absolute beginners?

Yes, as long as you know how to chain and double crochet, this pattern requires no complex shaping, decreases, or difficult stitch combinations.

Should I block my cover-up after finishing?

Wet blocking or light steam blocking is highly recommended because it opens up the mesh grid evenly, relaxes the yarn fibers, and defines the straight rectangular edges.
