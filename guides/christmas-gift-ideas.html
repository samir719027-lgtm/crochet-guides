What to buy or make, sorted by budget and how much time you actually have before December.

Christmas gifts for crocheters go wrong the same way any craft gift does — guessing at sizes, colours, or preferences the recipient never mentioned. This list sticks to safe bets, plus a section on what to make yourself if you're crocheting the gifts.

Timing tip: if you're making gifts rather than buying them, start simple projects by October and anything larger — blankets, garments — by September. Our general gift guide covers safe picks year-round if Christmas timing doesn't apply to you.

Gifts to make yourself

If you're crocheting the gift, stick to projects that don't depend on knowing the recipient's exact size:

  • Granny square coaster set — quick, finishes in an evening or two, and our granny square tutorial covers it step by step.
  • Simple scarf — one size fits everyone, and a single crochet scarf is forgiving for a beginner working to a deadline.
  • Small amigurumi keychain or ornament — low-commitment and finishes quickly; see our amigurumi guide if it's your first attempt.

Avoid starting a garment or anything requiring exact sizing unless you already know the recipient's measurements and have made something similar before — a rushed, ill-fitting gift is worse than a simpler one finished well.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start a crocheted Christmas gift?

For a beginner, starting in October gives comfortable time for a simple project like a scarf or granny square set, accounting for a few false starts. Larger projects like blankets or garments need more lead time, ideally starting by September.

What's a quick crochet gift for someone I'm not very close to?

A single granny square coaster set or a small amigurumi keychain both finish in a few hours and don't require knowing the recipient's exact sizing or colour preferences the way a garment would.

Should I buy or make a crochet gift for a crocheter?

Both work well. Buying notions, a stitch dictionary, or a hook set removes any guesswork about sizing or colour. Making something is more personal but only worth attempting if you're confident finishing it in time and to a standard you're happy with.
