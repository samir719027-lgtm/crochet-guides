Crochet Granny Square: Complete Beginner Tutorial

The exact stitches for your first granny square, written in US terms, from magic ring to finished square.

Updated 2026 · 7 min read

The granny square is one of the most-recognised crochet patterns for a reason: it only uses chain and double crochet, works in rounds without any tricky shaping, and teaches colour-changing and corner construction all in one small project. This tutorial covers three rounds, enough for a coaster or the start of a blanket square.

Written in US terms. If you're not sure what that means, our abbreviations glossary explains the difference between US and UK stitch names before you start.

What you need

  • A 5.0mm hook (see our hook buying guide if you don't have one)
  • Worsted-weight yarn in one or more colours
  • Scissors and a yarn needle for weaving in ends

Round 1: the centre

Steps

  1. Chain 4, then join into a ring with a slip stitch into the first chain.
  2. Chain 3 (this counts as your first double crochet).
  3. Work 2 more double crochet into the ring.
  4. Chain 2 (this forms the corner).
  5. Work [3 double crochet, chain 2] into the ring three more times — you'll have 4 groups of 3 double crochet, each separated by a chain-2 corner.
  6. Join with a slip stitch to the top of your starting chain-3. Fasten off if changing colour, or continue to Round 2.

Round 2: building the square

Steps

  1. Join your yarn (new colour or same) into any chain-2 corner space.
  2. Chain 3, then work [2 double crochet, chain 2, 3 double crochet] into that same corner space — this makes the corner.
  3. Chain 1, then work 3 double crochet into the next chain-2 space (this is now a side, not a corner).
  4. Repeat: corner in each corner space, chain 1 and 3 double crochet in each side space, going around all 4 sides.
  5. Join with a slip stitch to the top of your starting chain-3.

Round 3: repeat the pattern

Steps

Round 3 repeats the same logic as Round 2, just with one more side section per edge:

  1. Join into any corner space, chain 3, then [2 double crochet, chain 2, 3 double crochet] in that corner.
  2. Chain 1, 3 double crochet in the next space, chain 1, 3 double crochet in the next space (two side groups now, instead of one).
  3. Repeat around all 4 sides, then join with a slip stitch to close the round.

Every additional round follows this same pattern: a corner group in each corner, and one more side group per edge than the round before.

Finishing and joining squares

Fasten off and weave in your ends with a yarn needle. For a single coaster, you're done. To make a blanket, crochet multiple squares to the same round count, then join them either by whip stitching the edges together with a yarn needle, or by single crocheting them together as a border — whip stitching is the simpler option for a first attempt.

Frequently asked questions

How many rounds does a basic granny square have?

A basic granny square is often worked to 3 or 4 rounds for a coaster-sized square, roughly 4 to 5 inches across. You can keep adding rounds to make it as large as you like for a blanket.

Do I need to change yarn colour for each round of a granny square?

No, colour changes are a style choice, not a requirement. A granny square made in a single colour throughout is just as structurally correct as a multicoloured one.

How do I join granny squares together into a blanket?

The two most common methods are whip stitching the edges together with a yarn needle, or single crocheting the squares together as a border. Whip stitching is simpler for a first attempt; single crochet joining gives a neater, more finished edge.
