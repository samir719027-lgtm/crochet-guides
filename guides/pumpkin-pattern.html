Free Crochet Pumpkin Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

A ribbed amigurumi pumpkin using back-loop stitches for texture, with a simple stem on top.

Updated 2026 · US terms · About 4in / 10cm across

This pumpkin uses the same increase-and-decrease shaping as our mini amigurumi ball pattern, with back loop only stitches added through the body to create the ribbed, pumpkin-like texture. A quick seasonal decoration that uses very little yarn.

Materials

  • Small amount of orange worsted-weight yarn (about 30–40 yards)
  • Small amount of green or brown yarn, for the stem
  • 4.0mm hook
  • Polyester fiberfill stuffing
  • Stitch marker, scissors, and a yarn needle

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
scSingle crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)
inc2 sc in same stitch (increase)
decSc 2 sts together (decrease)
BLOBack loop only
rnd(s)Round(s)

Gauge

Gauge isn't critical for this small project — the goal is tight, even stitches that hide the stuffing rather than an exact measurement.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral, same shaping as our mini amigurumi ball pattern.
  • Working through the back loop only (BLO) on the body rounds is what creates the ribbed pumpkin texture.
  • A short crochet chain or a chenille stem substitute both work for the stem — see the FAQ below.

Pattern

Body, Round 1

Using orange yarn, make a magic ring, 6 sc into ring, place marker. (6 sts)

Body, Round 2

Inc in every st around. (12 sts)

Body, Round 3

[sc 1, inc] around. (18 sts)

Body, Round 4

Working in BLO throughout this round, [sc 2, inc] around. (24 sts)

Body, Rounds 5–7

Working in BLO throughout, sc in every st around, no increases. (24 sts each round)

Body, Round 8

Working in BLO throughout, [sc 2, dec] around. (18 sts)

Begin stuffing firmly.

Body, Round 9

Working in BLO throughout, [sc 1, dec] around. (12 sts)

Add final stuffing to fill evenly.

Stem

Switch to green or brown yarn. Chain 8, then sc in the 2nd chain from hook and in each remaining chain. Fasten off, leaving a short tail for sewing.

Finishing

Close the body opening by threading the orange tail through the front loops of the last round and pulling tight. Sew the stem securely into the centre of the top opening. Weave in all ends.

A beginner amigurumi tutorial for a crochet pumpkin, useful for seeing the ribbed shaping and stuffing technique.

Getting defined ribs: the back-loop texture shows up most clearly with a slightly tighter tension than usual. If the ribs look faint, try a hook one size smaller for the body rounds.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get the ribbed pumpkin shape in crochet?

The ribbed look comes from working back loop only stitches through most of the body rounds, which creates visible horizontal ridges similar to a real pumpkin's grooves.

What can I use for a pumpkin stem if I don't want to crochet one?

A short length of brown or green chenille stem (pipe cleaner), bent and trimmed to size, works as a quick substitute and can simply be pushed into the top opening before closing.

How many pumpkins does one skein of yarn make?

A single skein of worsted-weight yarn typically makes 2 to 3 pumpkins at this size, depending on the total yardage of the skein.
