Free Crochet Cowl Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

A closed loop worked in half double crochet, no ties or ends to manage — just slip it over your head.

Updated 2026 · US terms · One adult size, adjustable circumference

My first cowl came out with a permanent twist in it, a subtle mobius kink I didn't notice until I'd worked six rounds past the join. I wore it anyway for an entire winter before finally admitting the twist bothered me enough to start over. Now I always lay the chain flat on the table and run a finger along the whole length before joining — ten extra seconds that would have saved me a whole winter of a slightly crooked cowl.

This cowl is worked as a tube in continuous rounds, the same spiral technique as our beanie pattern, just left open at both ends instead of closed at the top. Half double crochet keeps it soft and warm without being bulky.

Materials

  • 2 skeins worsted-weight yarn (about 200–250 yards total)
  • 5.5mm (US size I) crochet hook
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means

Gauge

12 hdc and 10 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.5mm hook and worsted yarn. The finished circumference depends directly on your starting chain — measure loosely around the neck and shoulders before starting.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral — use a stitch marker to track round starts, same technique as our beanie pattern.
  • The numbers below make a single-loop cowl that sits at the collarbone; see the FAQ for how to size up to a double loop.
  • Check the starting chain against the wearer before joining into a round — it should slip over the head comfortably.

Pattern

Round 1

Ch 60, join with a sl st to form a ring, being careful not to twist the chain. Place marker. (60 sts)

Rounds 2–14

Hdc in every st around. (60 sts each round)

Continue until the cowl measures about 9in / 23cm tall, roughly 14 rounds for this gauge.

Finishing

Fasten off and weave in all ends with a yarn needle.

Sizing to fit: before joining round 1, wrap the chain loosely around the neck and shoulders to check it slips over the head comfortably. Add or remove chains in multiples of 2 to adjust.

Changing the drape

Half double crochet gives this cowl a soft middle ground — warmer and denser than a lacy stitch, but with more movement than a stiff single crochet fabric. If you want a cowl that hangs and folds more like a scarf, swap in double crochet at the same stitch count; the taller stitch opens the fabric up and lets it drape rather than stand away from the body. For a chunkier, more structured cowl that holds its shape well, single crochet in the same rounds gives a denser result, though it will use noticeably more yarn for the same finished height.

A simple mesh pattern (alternating chain-1 spaces with double crochet) is another easy swap for a lighter, more breathable cowl suited to milder weather, without changing the basic construction of joining a tube and working rounds straight up.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the starting chain gets a twist in it before joining, leaving a permanent kink running through the finished cowl.
Fix: lay the chain flat on a table and run a finger along its entire length to check for a twist before joining with the slip stitch — it's much easier to catch and fix before round 1 than after several rounds are worked.
Mistake: the finished loop is too tight to pull over the head, or so loose it slides down constantly.
Fix: wrap the starting chain loosely around the neck and shoulders before joining, adding or removing chains in multiples of 2 until it slips over comfortably with a little room to spare.
Mistake: the cowl comes out stiffer and more structured than expected, standing away from the neck instead of draping.
Fix: switch to a taller, more open stitch like double crochet or a mesh pattern, described above, which drapes more naturally than dense half double or single crochet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a cowl and a scarf?

A cowl is a closed loop that slips over the head, while a scarf is a flat, open strip that gets wrapped and tied. Cowls tend to stay in place better and have no loose ends to manage.

How much yarn do I need for a crochet cowl?

A single-loop cowl uses roughly 200 to 250 yards of worsted-weight yarn, comfortably within two standard skeins.

Can I make this cowl pattern longer for a double loop?

Yes, work more rounds before joining to create a longer tube that wraps twice around the neck, roughly doubling the round count and yarn needed.

How do I stop a crochet cowl from twisting when I join it into a loop?

Lay the starting chain flat on a table before joining and run a finger along its full length to check for a twist, since even a half-turn will show up as a permanent kink once the round is joined and worked.

What crochet stitch gives a cowl the best drape?

Taller, more open stitches like double crochet or a simple mesh pattern drape better than dense single crochet, which tends to sit stiffer and more structured around the neck.
