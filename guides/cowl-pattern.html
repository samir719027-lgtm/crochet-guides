Free Crochet Cowl Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

A closed loop worked in half double crochet, no ties or ends to manage — just slip it over your head.

Updated 2026 · US terms · One adult size, adjustable circumference

This cowl is worked as a tube in continuous rounds, the same spiral technique as our beanie pattern, just left open at both ends instead of closed at the top. Half double crochet keeps it soft and warm without being bulky.

Materials

  • 2 skeins worsted-weight yarn (about 200–250 yards total)
  • 5.5mm (US size I) crochet hook
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
chChain
hdcHalf double crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)
rnd(s)Round(s)

See our abbreviations glossary for the full list including UK terms.

Gauge

12 hdc and 10 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.5mm hook and worsted yarn. The finished circumference depends directly on your starting chain — measure loosely around the neck and shoulders before starting.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral — use a stitch marker to track round starts, same technique as our beanie pattern.
  • The numbers below make a single-loop cowl that sits at the collarbone; see the FAQ for how to size up to a double loop.
  • Check the starting chain against the wearer before joining into a round — it should slip over the head comfortably.

Pattern

Round 1

Ch 60, join with a sl st to form a ring, being careful not to twist the chain. Place marker. (60 sts)

Rounds 2–14

Hdc in every st around. (60 sts each round)

Continue until the cowl measures about 9in / 23cm tall, roughly 14 rounds for this gauge.

Finishing

Fasten off and weave in all ends with a yarn needle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a cowl and a scarf?

A cowl is a closed loop that slips over the head, while a scarf is a flat, open strip that gets wrapped and tied. Cowls tend to stay in place better and have no loose ends to manage.

How much yarn do I need for a crochet cowl?

A single-loop cowl uses roughly 200 to 250 yards of worsted-weight yarn, comfortably within two standard skeins.

Can I make this cowl pattern longer for a double loop?

Yes, work more rounds before joining to create a longer tube that wraps twice around the neck, roughly doubling the round count and yarn needed.
