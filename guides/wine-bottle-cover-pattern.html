Free Crochet Wine Bottle Cover Pattern for Beginners

A reusable, handmade gift topper built from the same increase-and-decrease shaping as our amigurumi patterns, sized for a full bottle.

Updated 2026 · US terms · Fits a standard 750ml bottle

I started making wine bottle covers the Christmas a friend asked how to make a bottle of wine feel like a handmade gift without actually brewing the wine myself. The cover was the answer — twenty minutes of crochet that turns a shop-bought bottle into something that looks wrapped by hand, and the recipient gets to keep it after the wine's gone. In my classes I use this crochet wine bottle cover pattern to teach shaping, because it's a flat circle that becomes a tube, then cinches in with a few decreases to hug the neck. That one project sneaks in three separate techniques, and nobody notices they're learning because it's finished so quickly.

The neat thing about a bottle cover is that the bottle does half the measuring for you. Keep slipping the cover over the actual bottle as you work and you'll catch a poor fit in round one instead of round twenty. I've watched students fiddle with tape measures for ten minutes when all they needed was the bottle sitting right there on the table. Fit it to the thing it's wrapping and the whole project becomes almost foolproof.

Materials

  • 1 skein worsted-weight yarn (about 60–80 yards)
  • 5.0mm (US size H) crochet hook
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Gauge

14 sc and 16 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.0mm hook and worsted yarn. Gauge affects fit here — check the base circle against the bottom of your bottle before continuing to the sides.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral — use a stitch marker to track round starts, same technique as our plant hanger pattern. If a magic ring is still new to you, our magic ring tutorial covers the starting loop in detail.
  • Sized for a standard 750ml wine bottle; add a round or two to the body for a champagne bottle.
  • The neck decreases are what let the cover hug the bottle rather than slide off.

Pattern

Base, Round 1

Make a magic ring, 6 sc into ring, place marker. (6 sts)

Base, Round 2

Inc in every st around. (12 sts)

Base, Round 3

[sc 1, inc] around. (18 sts)

Base, Round 4

[sc 2, inc] around. (24 sts)

Body, Rounds 5–18

Sc in every st around for 14 rounds, no increases. (24 sts each round)

Check the body height against your bottle periodically; add or remove rounds here to match the bottle's shoulder height.

Neck, Round 19

[sc 2, dec] around. (18 sts)

Neck, Round 20

[sc 1, dec] around. (12 sts)

Neck, Rounds 21–23

Sc in every st around for 3 rounds, no further decreases. (12 sts each round)

Finishing

Fasten off and weave in all ends with a yarn needle.

Yarn & colour choice

Anything smooth and worsted works here, but cotton is my pick for a gift topper because it doesn't fuzz against the glass and keeps its shape while it's slid on and off. Wool is warmer-looking for a winter bottle, though it will felt if it ever ends up in hot water. Avoid super-stretchy or heavily textured yarns, which make the neck decreases sloppy and stop the cover from hugging the bottle. For colour, think about the occasion rather than the room: a deep red or cream reads as Christmas, a soft neutral suits a housewarming, and a bright leftover makes a birthday bottle look deliberate. Fitting bottles beyond the standard 750ml

The same base-and-tube shaping adapts to almost anything cylindrical. A champagne bottle is wider at the shoulder, so add a round or two of increases to the base and a couple of extra body rounds to reach the higher slope. Olive-oil or vinegar bottles usually have a shorter neck, so stop the neck decreases a round earlier and check against the bottle. I've even stretched the pattern over a tall glass vase by working more body rounds and skipping the neck cinch entirely. The rule that never changes is the same as our guide to working in the round: mark your start and test the fit often, because a bottle cover that fits is the whole point.

The reusable gift wrap

This is my favourite trick for a last-minute gift that still feels considered. Slip a cover over a bottle of wine, olive oil, or a fancy bottle of fizzy cordial, and the wrapping becomes part of the present — the recipient keeps it long after the contents are gone. It saves a sheet of paper, hides a shop-bought label, and tells the person you spent real time on them. For an extra touch, tie a contrasting ribbon at the neck over the cover, or make the cover in the giftee's kitchen colours so it becomes a permanent fixture rather than a once-a-year decoration. Work a matching one in the same cotton for a neighbour and you've got the start of a small handmade set.

Common mistakes & fixes

Cover slides off the neck: the neck decreases are too loose. Test the fit around the bottle's neck as you work rounds 19–23 and ease the decreases tighter if it feels baggy.
Won't fit over the bottom of the bottle: the base circle is too small. Check the flat circle against the bottle's base before starting the body rounds, and add a round of increases if it's short.
Neck won't reach over the foil or cork: the neck section ended too low. Add a round or two to rounds 21–23 and test the cover over the top of the bottle before fastening off.
Body sags or wrinkles: tension was too loose through the straight rounds. Drop a hook size for the body so the tube holds its shape against the glass instead of slouching.
Visible slanted seam spirals up the side: that's the natural jog of a continuous spiral, not a mistake. Keep the stitch marker in place so your rounds stay even, and remember the slant will read as texture once it's on a bottle.
Stitches too tight to stretch over the shoulder: you overcorrected for fit. Aim for snug rather than tight — the cover should slide on with a light pull, not need to be wrestled over the widest point.

Frequently asked questions

How much yarn do I need for a wine bottle cover?

A single bottle cover uses roughly 60 to 80 yards of worsted-weight yarn, comfortably within one skein with plenty left over.

Does this pattern fit a standard wine bottle?

Yes, this pattern is sized for a standard 750ml wine bottle. For a champagne bottle or a different shape, add a round or two to the body before starting the neck decreases.

Can I use a crochet wine bottle cover as a gift wrap alternative?

Yes, it's a popular reusable alternative to paper gift wrap for bottle gifts, and the recipient keeps a handmade cover afterward rather than throwing away wrapping paper.

Can I make a wine bottle cover for a champagne or sparkler bottle?

Yes, champagne bottles are wider, so add a round or two of increases to the base before starting the straight body rounds, and work a couple more body rounds to reach the higher shoulder. Test the fit against the bottle as you go.

What other bottles can I use this cover pattern for?

The same shaping fits most 750ml bottles and can be adapted to oil or vinegar bottles, soap dispensers, or even a tall vase by adding or removing body rounds to match the height and widening or narrowing the base to suit the bottle's diameter.

How do I stop the cover from sliding off the bottle?

Make the neck decreases snug rather than tight and test them against the bottle's neck as you go. A cover that hugs the narrow part of the bottle stays put; one that's loose there will slip down with handling.

Can I wash a crochet wine bottle cover?

Yes — hand-wash in cool water and reshape it over the bottle or a rolled towel to dry. If it's wool, use cool water and no agitation to avoid felting.
