Amigurumi supply lists can look long, but most of what actually matters fits into four categories: a smaller hook, stuffing, safety eyes, and a couple of small finishing tools. If you've already read our amigurumi for beginners guide, this is the shopping list version.

Quick answer: if you're buying just one thing today, get a smaller hook (2.5–3.5mm). It's the single change that makes the biggest difference between amigurumi that looks tidy and amigurumi with visible gaps.

What you can skip for now

  • Amigurumi-specific hooks. Marketing aside, a standard hook in the right smaller size does the same job as one labelled "for amigurumi."
  • Specialty stuffing tools. A chopstick or the blunt end of a pencil works fine for pushing stuffing into small spaces — a dedicated stuffing tool is a nice-to-have, not a requirement.
  • Full colour range of safety eyes. One assorted mixed-size pack covers most projects; buy specific colours only once you know you need them.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need special hooks for amigurumi?

Not special hooks specifically, just a smaller size than you'd normally use, typically 2.5mm to 3.5mm. A standard ergonomic hook set that includes those sizes works fine, no dedicated amigurumi-branded hook is required.

What stitch markers work best for amigurumi?

Locking stitch markers are preferred over open ring markers, since amigurumi is worked in continuous spiral rounds and a marker that can slip off mid-project is more disruptive than one that locks closed.

Is a yarn needle different from a sewing needle for amigurumi?

Yes, yarn needles have a larger eye and a blunt tip, designed to pass through crochet stitches without splitting the yarn. A sharp sewing needle can split fibres and make seams look uneven.
