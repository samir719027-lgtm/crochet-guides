Free Car Seat Blanket Pattern (Harness Strap Slots)

Pin this pattern

A cream blanket with five harness strap slots worked right into the fabric, so the straps pass through onto the baby instead of bunching underneath.

Updated 2026 · US terms · About 20 x 30in / 51 x 76cm

I designed this pattern after a frustrating afternoon trying to make a regular blanket work with a car seat harness, tucking it awkwardly around the straps and watching it bunch up under the baby within five minutes. The actual fix turned out to be obvious once I thought about it properly: build the strap openings directly into the blanket, so the harness passes through the fabric instead of fighting against it.

The blanket itself is a straightforward flat rectangle worked in rows of double crochet, nothing complicated there. The real design work is in the five buttonhole-style slots positioned to match a standard 5-point harness: two at the shoulders, two at the hips, and one at the crotch. Once it's in place, the straps thread straight through and buckle exactly as they would without the blanket there at all.

Below is the full pattern in US terms, along with a clear note on how this fits into car seat safety guidance, sizing for infant versus convertible seats, and the mistakes that make the slots misalign with a real harness. If you'd rather a blanket without strap slots for general use, our easy baby blanket pattern is a good alternative, and if you're new to picking a hook for a given yarn weight, our hook size chart is worth a look before you start.

A note on car seat safety: a thin, flat layer that the harness passes directly through, like this blanket's slots, is different from bulky padding placed under or behind a baby before the harness is buckled. Car seat safety guidance generally advises against bulky aftermarket padding between a baby and the harness, since it compresses in a crash and leaves the harness too loose to do its job. This blanket is designed to sit under the harness with the straps threaded through the slots directly onto the baby, not as padding hidden underneath them. Always check that the harness is snug and correctly positioned after adding this blanket, the same as you would every time you buckle a baby in.

Materials

  • About 600–750 yards of worsted-weight yarn
  • 5mm (US H-8) crochet hook
  • Yarn needle and scissors
  • Stitch marker

See our abbreviations glossary for the full list including UK terms.

Gauge

13 dc and 8 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5mm hook and worsted yarn.

Notes

  • Worked flat in rows of double crochet, with 5 slots added at specific rows for the harness straps.
  • Slot positions given fit most standard infant and convertible seats; check the fit against your own seat before finishing (see the fitting section).
  • Finished size is sized for an infant or convertible car seat, not a full-size crib blanket.

Pattern

Foundation

Chain 43. Double crochet in the 4th chain from the hook, and in each chain across. Turn. (40 sts)

Rows 1–10

Chain 2 (does not count as a stitch), double crochet in each stitch across. Turn. Repeat for 10 rows total. (40 sts each row)

Row 11 (shoulder slots)

Chain 2, double crochet in the first 10 stitches. Skip the next 3 stitches, chain 3 over the gap (first shoulder slot). Double crochet in the next 14 stitches. Skip the next 3 stitches, chain 3 over the gap (second shoulder slot). Double crochet in the remaining 10 stitches. Turn.

Row 12

Chain 2, double crochet in each stitch and each chain-3 space across, treating each 3-chain space as 3 stitches. (40 sts) This closes the top of each slot, leaving a clean buttonhole-style opening in the fabric below.

Rows 13–21

Chain 2, double crochet in each stitch across. Turn. Repeat for 9 rows.

Row 22 (hip slots)

Repeat Row 11's slot placement exactly: double crochet 10, slot, double crochet 14, slot, double crochet 10. Turn.

Row 23

Repeat Row 12, closing both hip slots the same way.

Rows 24–31

Chain 2, double crochet in each stitch across. Turn. Repeat for 8 rows.

Row 32 (crotch slot)

Chain 2, double crochet in the first 18 stitches. Skip the next 4 stitches, chain 4 over the gap (crotch slot, slightly wider than the others). Double crochet in the remaining 18 stitches. Turn.

Row 33 and remaining rows

Chain 2, double crochet in each stitch and the chain-4 space across, closing the crotch slot. (40 sts) Continue even in double crochet until the piece measures about 30in / 76cm total. Fasten off.

Border

Work 1 round of single crochet evenly around the entire outer edge, working 3 single crochet in each corner. Join, fasten off, weave in ends.

Fitting the slots to your seat

Harness spacing genuinely varies between car seat models and even between infant and convertible seats from the same manufacturer, so treat the slot positions above as a solid starting layout rather than a universal guarantee. Before working row 33 and finishing the blanket, lay the piece across your actual car seat and check that the shoulder, hip, and crotch slots line up reasonably well with where your harness straps come through the seat shell. If a slot is noticeably off, it's straightforward to frog back a few rows and reposition it before you commit to the rest of the blanket.

Because babies grow and harness height adjusts over time, don't expect the slots to align perfectly forever; this blanket is sized for the early infant-seat stage, and a slight mismatch as your baby grows into the next harness height is normal and not a sign you did anything wrong.

Sizing for infant vs. convertible seats

The dimensions above, about 20 x 30in / 51 x 76cm, are built around a rear-facing infant seat used roughly from newborn through 9-12 months, which is the stage where the harness sits low and the seat shell is narrowest. If you know you'll be using this on a larger convertible seat from the start, whether for a baby closer to 6-9 months or a toddler who has already moved up, add 2-3 extra rows before row 1 begins and repeat that same extra row count again after row 33's plain rows, so the slot positions still land at the same one-third and two-thirds marks proportionally. This keeps the shoulder, hip, and crotch slots aligned with a wider harness spread without having to redesign the slot rows themselves.

For a newborn in a bucket-style infant seat, some parents prefer to size down slightly, working the foundation chain 3-4 stitches narrower and shortening the plain rows between slots by a row or two each, since a newborn's shoulder-to-hip harness spacing is noticeably shorter than a 9-month-old's. There's no single correct size here the way there is with a fitted garment; the fitting check against your actual seat described above matters more than hitting an exact age-range number.

Choosing yarn and hook size

A worsted-weight yarn with a 5mm hook gives a fabric with enough body to hold the slot openings cleanly without being bulky against the harness. Choose a soft, machine-washable fiber, since this blanket needs regular washing given how much contact it gets. Our yarn weight guide covers fiber choices if you're substituting a different yarn.

Care & washing

Machine wash on a gentle cycle and lay flat to dry, checking that the slots haven't stretched out of shape before the next use. A car seat blanket gets more handling and washing than almost any other baby item on this site, so it's worth choosing a genuinely durable, machine-washable yarn from the start rather than something delicate.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the slots don't line up with the actual harness straps once the blanket is finished.
Fix: check slot placement against your real car seat before working the final rows, as described in the fitting section, rather than trusting the given measurements blindly.
Mistake: a slot stretches out of shape or gapes open after repeated use.
Fix: work the closing row over each chain-space snugly rather than loosely, treating the chain stitches exactly like regular stitches so the slot keeps a defined edge.
Mistake: the two shoulder slots (or the two hip slots) end up at different heights.
Fix: work the slot row and its closing row in a single sitting if possible, and double-check your stitch count before and after each slot to catch any miscount early.
Mistake: the blanket bunches up under the baby despite having strap slots.
Fix: make sure the harness is actually threaded through the slots rather than just laid over the top of the blanket; the whole design only works when the straps pass through the fabric itself.
Mistake: the finished blanket is noticeably too wide or narrow for the car seat.
Fix: measure your specific seat's harness width before starting, and adjust the foundation chain in multiples of 3 (to keep the pattern's proportions consistent) if your seat is significantly different from a standard size.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to put a blanket under a car seat harness?

A thin, flat layer that the harness passes directly through, like this pattern's strap slots, is different from bulky padding placed under or behind a baby before the harness is buckled. Car seat safety guidance generally advises against bulky padding between a baby and the harness, since it compresses in a crash and leaves the harness too loose. This blanket is designed to sit under the harness with the straps passing through the slots directly onto the baby, not as padding underneath them.

How do the strap slots work?

Five buttonhole-style slots are worked directly into the blanket body during construction, positioned to match a standard 5-point harness: two at the shoulders, two at the hips, and one at the crotch. The harness straps pass through these slots so the blanket sits between the baby and the harness rather than underneath it.

Will this fit any car seat?

The slot spacing given is a general-purpose layout that fits most infant and convertible seats reasonably well, but harness spacing does vary between models. Check the fit against your own seat before finishing, and adjust slot placement if needed; the note in the sizing section covers how.

Can I use this as a regular blanket too, not just in the car seat?

Yes. Outside the car seat, it works as a normal small blanket for supervised use, a stroller cover, or tummy time. The strap slots don't interfere with any other use.

What yarn and hook size should I use?

A worsted-weight yarn with a 5mm hook gives a fabric with enough body to hold the slot shapes cleanly without being bulky against the harness. Choose a soft, machine-washable fiber.

Can I wash a car seat blanket in the machine?

Yes, as long as the yarn is machine-washable. Wash on a gentle cycle and lay flat to dry, checking that the slots haven't stretched out of shape before the next use.
