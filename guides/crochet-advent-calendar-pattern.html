Crochet Advent Calendar Pattern (Mini Stocking Garland)

Pin this pattern

Twelve tiny crochet stockings, strung on a garland, each one ready to fill with a small treat.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 2 hr read (one evening per few stockings)

This advent-style garland is built from small stocking shapes — far quicker than the full-size Christmas stocking pattern — repeated and strung along a length of twine. Each mini stocking is just big enough to hold a wrapped candy or a small folded note.

Quick answer: Crochet one small stocking shape repeatedly (12 for a full countdown, or fewer for a shorter garland), add a hanging loop to each, then string them all along a piece of twine or ribbon.

What you need

  • Worsted or DK weight yarn in 3 alternating colors (red, cream, green work well)
  • 4.0–4.5mm hook
  • A length of twine, cotton cord, or ribbon for the garland base
  • Yarn needle
  • Small clothespins or a needle and thread to attach each stocking to the garland

One mini stocking (repeat 12 times)

Steps

  1. Chain 8. Work sc in the 2nd chain from hook and each chain across. (7 sts) Chain 1, turn.
  2. Work 3 rows even in sc.
  3. Row 5 (heel/toe corner): work 2 sc in the first stitch, sc across. (8 sts) Chain 1, turn.
  4. Row 6: sc across evenly.
  5. Row 7 (toe point): sc2tog, sc to last 2 sts, sc2tog. (6 sts) Fasten off, leaving a tail.
  6. Fold the piece in half along its length and whip-stitch the two side edges together, leaving the top open, to form a tiny stocking pouch.

Hanging loop

Steps

  1. With the same or a contrast color, chain 6 at the top back corner of the stocking.
  2. Slip stitch back into the same spot to form a small loop, fasten off, and weave in ends.

Assembling the garland

Steps

  1. Make 12 stockings total (or as many as you'd like), alternating colors so no two neighbors match.
  2. Lay out your twine or ribbon to the length you want the garland to hang.
  3. Thread each stocking's hanging loop onto the twine, or clip it on with a small clothespin, spacing them evenly.
  4. Optional: number each stocking with fabric marker, a small tag, or a different stitch pattern so it works as a true 1–24 or 1–12 countdown.

A demonstration of making small numbered pockets, useful for seeing how each piece is sized and finished.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make 24 for a full December countdown?

Yes — the mini stocking is quick enough to repeat 24 times; just plan for roughly double the yarn and time.

What can I put inside each stocking?

Wrapped candies, a small ornament, a folded note with an activity for the day, or a tiny toy all fit well.

Do I need to number them?

Only if you want a strict day-by-day countdown — without numbers, it still works beautifully as a general holiday garland.
