Cozy Crochet Hooded Scarf Pattern

Pin this guide

Keep winter drafts at bay with this clever, two-in-one crochet hooded scarf that pairs a cozy neck cowl with a generous, warm hood.

Updated 2026 · 8 min read

There is something wonderful about wearable accessories that pull double duty when the temperature drops. A hooded scarf gives you the best of both worlds: the snug warmth of a traditional neck cowl combined with a deep, comfortable hood that you can simply flip up when the wind picks up. Over my twelve years of teaching, I have found this project to be a favorite among crafters who want a practical garment without the complexity of traditional sweater construction.

This pattern uses basic US crochet stitches and a bulky yarn to work up surprisingly fast. You will start by working the long scarf section flat, fold and seam the hood portion, and then seamlessly transition into the neck cowl. I will walk you through each construction step in plain English, ensuring you end up with a polished, wearable winter staple you will reach for year after year.

Quick answer: A crochet hooded scarf is made by working a long rectangular base, seaming a portion of it to form the hood, and joining the ends into a cozy cowl. It uses basic stitches like half double crochet and bulky yarn for quick, warm results.

Materials and Gauge

To make this hooded scarf, you will need approximately 650 yards of bulky (weight 5) yarn and a 6.5 mm (K-10.5) crochet hook. Bulky yarn gives the fabric enough structure to hold its hood shape without feeling stiff or heavy around your neck. Always check your gauge before diving in, as slouchy wearables depend heavily on the right drape and tension.

Working a small 4-inch swatch in half double crochet will save you from sizing surprises later. If your stitches are too tight, your hood will pull awkwardly; if they are too loose, the fabric will stretch out and lose its warmth. Take ten minutes to measure your work—it is time well spent on any garment project.

  • 650 yards bulky weight yarn
  • 6.5 mm crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle for weaving ends
  • Stitch markers to keep your place

Working the Scarf Foundation

We begin at the bottom end of the scarf. Chain 24 loosely to set your width. For your foundation row, work a half double crochet in the third chain from the hook and in each chain across, then chain 2 and turn. This creates a sturdy, neat edge that will not curl or warp with regular wear.

Continue working rows of half double crochet until your piece measures roughly 60 inches in length. This long span allows you to wrap the ends comfortably around your neck once the hood is up. Keep your tension even across these rows so the scarf drapes naturally against your coat.

  • Ch 24 loosely
  • Hdc in 3rd ch from hook and across
  • Repeat hdc rows until piece reaches 60 inches

Shaping and Seaming the Hood

Once your 60-inch scarf panel is complete, lay it flat on a table to assemble the hood. Fold one end of the scarf inward by about 14 inches to form a neat right angle at the top back of the head. Pin this fold in place with stitch markers so it does not shift while you work.

Using your tapestry needle and a length of matching yarn, seam along the back edge of the fold using a simple mattress stitch or whip stitch. This creates the rounded crown of your hood. Fasten off securely and weave in your tails before trying it on for fit.

  • Fold 14 inches of scarf end inward
  • Align raw edges carefully
  • Seam the back crown using a whip stitch

Adding the Cowl Collar

The final step turns the remaining fabric into a structured, gap-free neck warmer. Pick up stitches evenly around the front opening of the hood and upper scarf section. Work three rounds of textured stitches, such as alternating front and back post half double crochets, to give the neckline a polished ribbing.

Finish off by cutting your yarn, leaving a six-inch tail, and weaving it neatly into the wrong side of the fabric. Give your finished hooded scarf a gentle steam block according to your yarn label instructions to relax the stitches and set the final shape.

  • Pick up stitches around the neck opening
  • Work 3 rounds of ribbed texture
  • Fasten off and weave in all yarn tails

Frequently asked questions

Can I use worsted weight yarn instead of bulky yarn?

Yes, but you will need to hold two strands of worsted weight yarn together or adjust your stitch count significantly to achieve the same warmth and structure.

How do I adjust the size of the hood?

You can make the hood deeper by folding a longer section of the scarf before seaming, or shallower by folding less fabric.

Is this pattern suitable for complete beginners?

If you know how to chain and half double crochet, you can make this project. The assembly is simply a straight fold and a basic seam.

How should I wash my finished hooded scarf?

Check your yarn label first, but most acrylic or wool-blend hooded scarves benefit from a gentle hand wash and flat air drying.

Can I add a faux fur pom-pom to the hood?

A pom-pom makes a charming addition! Just attach it securely to the top corner seam once the hood construction is complete.
