Free Crochet Toddler Beanie Pattern for Beginners

A scaled-down version of our adult beanie — same technique, sized for a small head.

Updated 2026 · US terms · Ages 1–3, about 18–19in / 46–48cm head circumference

My nephew went through beanies faster than I could keep up with for about two years straight — not because he lost them, but because his head genuinely outgrew each one within a few months. That's what pushed me to figure out this scaled-down version properly instead of just eyeballing a smaller crown, and it's why the notes below lean so heavily on measuring rather than going by age labels alone.

If you've made the adult beanie pattern already, the technique here is identical, just stopped earlier. This guide covers the pattern plus the details that matter most for toddler wear: getting a reliable fit, safely adding flaps or a pom-pom, and choosing yarn that holds up to how toddlers actually treat their clothes.

This is a direct scale-down of our adult beanie pattern, stopping the crown increases two rounds earlier and working fewer straight rounds for a smaller circumference. If you've already made the adult version, the technique here is identical.

Materials

  • 1 skein worsted-weight yarn (about 100–120 yards)
  • 5.0mm (US size H) crochet hook
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
scSingle crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)
inc2 sc in same stitch (increase)
rnd(s)Round(s)

See our abbreviations glossary for the full list including UK terms.

Gauge

14 sc and 16 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.0mm hook and worsted yarn. Fit depends more on gauge at this size than in an adult beanie — measure the toddler's head before starting if possible.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral — use a stitch marker to track round starts, same as our adult beanie pattern.
  • Toddler head sizes vary more than adult sizes. Measure and compare against the crown circle before starting the straight rounds if an exact fit matters.
  • This pattern stops crown increases at round 6 (adult version continues to round 8), which is the main difference between the two sizes.

Pattern

Round 1

Make a magic ring, 6 sc into ring, place marker. (6 sts)

Round 2

Inc in every st around. (12 sts)

Round 3

[sc 1, inc] around. (18 sts)

Round 4

[sc 2, inc] around. (24 sts)

Round 5

[sc 3, inc] around. (30 sts)

Round 6

[sc 4, inc] around. (36 sts)

Rounds 7–14

Sc in every st around, no increases. Work 8 rounds straight, or until the hat measures about 1.5–2in / 4–5cm less than the toddler's head circumference — single crochet fabric stretches slightly once worn.

Finishing

Slip stitch into the next stitch to close, fasten off leaving a 6in / 15cm tail. Weave in ends with a yarn needle.

Sizing reliably

Toddler head sizes vary more between individual children than adult sizes do at the same "age" label, so treating an age range as a firm number is one of the most common ways this pattern comes out wrong. Measuring the toddler's actual head circumference, then comparing the flat crown circle against that measurement before starting the straight rounds, catches a sizing problem while it's still cheap to fix — unraveling six rounds of increases is a much smaller loss than unraveling the whole hat.

Because single crochet fabric has some stretch once worn, working the straight rounds to about 1.5-2in less than the measured head circumference (as the pattern notes) usually gives the most accurate real-world fit, rather than matching the measurement exactly and ending up with a hat that's slightly loose.

If you're making a hat as a gift without the toddler present to measure, it's safer to size slightly generous rather than snug. A hat with a little extra room can still be worn (perhaps folded up at the brim), while a hat that's genuinely too small can't be worn at all until it's unraveled and reworked. When in doubt, ask the parent for the toddler's current hat size or a rough age, and lean toward the larger end of that range.

Ear flaps & pom-poms

Ear flaps add real warmth for outdoor wear in cold climates and are simple to add without changing the crown construction: work two small triangular panels separately (decreasing a stitch or two each row from a starting width of about 8-10 stitches), then attach them to the brim edge on either side before working a border round around the whole opening, flaps included.

A pom-pom on top is a classic finishing touch, but it's worth thinking about the toddler's age before adding one as a separate piece. For younger or teething toddlers, sewing the pom-pom on securely with several passes of yarn is safer than a clip-on pom-pom, which can detach with enough pulling and become a choking hazard; an alternative is skipping a separate pom-pom altogether and finishing the crown with a simple gathered point instead.

A simple stripe pattern is another easy customization that doesn't add any safety considerations at all: switching yarn colors every 2-3 rounds during the straight section creates clean horizontal bands with no extra technique beyond a basic color change at the start of a round. It's an easy way to use up leftover yarn from other projects and gives each hat in a set of siblings' beanies its own distinct look.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: sizing by the age label on the pattern rather than the toddler's actual head measurement.
Fix: measure the head directly and compare it to the flat crown circle before continuing to the straight rounds — toddler sizes vary too much for an age range alone to be reliable.
Mistake: working the straight rounds to match the head measurement exactly, without accounting for the natural stretch of single crochet fabric.
Fix: stop the straight rounds about 1.5-2in short of the measured circumference — the fabric stretches slightly once worn, so this usually lands closer to a true snug fit.
Mistake: attaching a pom-pom or other decoration with just a few loose stitches, which can work loose with a toddler's handling.
Fix: sew any decoration on securely with multiple passes of yarn, or skip a separate piece entirely for younger toddlers who still put things in their mouths.
Mistake: using a scratchy or hand-wash-only yarn that a busy parent won't want to deal with.
Fix: choose a soft, machine-washable acrylic or acrylic blend — toddler items get washed often, and comfort against the skin matters more here than with most adult accessories.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scale an adult beanie pattern down for a toddler?

Stop the crown increase rounds two rounds earlier than an adult version, and work fewer straight rounds for the body, checking the flat crown circle against the toddler's head measurement as you go.

What head size does this toddler beanie fit?

This pattern is sized for an average 1 to 3 year old head circumference, roughly 18 to 19 inches. Measure the toddler's head for the most accurate fit, since sizes vary.

Is single crochet fabric warm enough for a toddler beanie?

Yes, single crochet produces a dense, tightly-worked fabric that blocks wind well. For extra warmth in very cold climates, a wool or wool-blend yarn adds insulation that acrylic doesn't provide.

Can I add ear flaps to this toddler beanie?

Yes — ear flaps are typically added by working two small triangular panels separately and joining them to the brim edge before adding a border round, rather than changing the crown construction itself.

Is it safe to add a pom-pom to a toddler's beanie?

For younger or teething toddlers, it's safer to skip a separate pom-pom or use one that's securely sewn on with multiple passes of yarn rather than a clip-on pom-pom, since a loose or removable one is a potential choking hazard.

How do I make this beanie last through more than one growth spurt?

Working the straight rounds slightly longer than the exact head measurement, and choosing a stretchy single crochet gauge rather than a very tight one, buys a bit more wear before the hat feels too small.
