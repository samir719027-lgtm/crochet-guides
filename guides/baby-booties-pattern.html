Free Crochet Baby Booties Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

A simple sole-and-sides construction, worked flat, for a quick and genuinely useful baby gift.

Updated 2026 · US terms · Fits 0–6 months

Booties were the first baby gift I ever made, for a friend's shower, and I finished the pair the night before without once checking the size against anything — they turned out to fit a newborn doll better than her actual six-month-old. She was gracious about it, but I've measured against a reference sole ever since. If you're making these for a specific baby, it's worth doing the same before you commit to a whole pair.

These booties use the same increase-and-flat-round technique from our beanie pattern, just scaled down and shaped for a foot instead of a head. They're a fast, practical project — a genuinely useful handmade gift that takes one evening rather than a full weekend.

Safety note: keep any decorative additions (buttons, bows) securely sewn on and appropriate for the child's age, or skip them entirely for very young infants. See the FAQ below for more on safe construction choices.

Materials

  • 1 skein soft, machine-washable worsted-weight yarn labelled safe for babies (about 60–80 yards)
  • 4.0mm (US size G) crochet hook
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Gauge

14 sc and 16 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 4.0mm hook and worsted yarn. Gauge affects fit more here than in larger projects — if your finished sole runs bigger or smaller than about 4in / 10cm long, adjust hook size before making a pair.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral — same technique as our beanie pattern. Use a stitch marker to track round starts.
  • Makes one bootie; repeat the entire pattern once more for a matching pair.
  • For a slightly larger size, add one more increase round to the sole before continuing to the sides.

Pattern (make 2)

Sole, Round 1

Ch 9. Starting in 2nd ch from hook, sc 7, work 3 sc in last ch, then sc 7 down the other side of the chain, work 2 sc in the last ch. Place marker. (18 sts)

Sole, Round 2

Inc, sc 6, inc 3 times, sc 6, inc twice. (24 sts)

Sole, Round 3

Sc in every st around, no increases. (24 sts)

Sides, Rounds 4–9

Sc in every st around for 6 rounds, working through the back loop only on round 4 for a defined edge between sole and sides. (24 sts each round)

Finishing the top opening

Fasten off, leaving a long tail. Weave the tail through the front loops of the last round and pull gently to slightly gather the opening, leaving enough space to fit over a baby's foot. Weave in the end.

Optional ankle strap: for older babies, chain 10 from one side of the top opening, skip to the opposite side, and sl st to attach, then work sc across the chain for a simple strap. Sew a button on securely if desired, checking it's tightly fastened.

Sizing for older babies

The pattern as written (Ch 9, 18 starting stitches) fits a newborn to about 6 months, roughly a 3.5–4in / 9–10cm foot. For each size up, add 2 chains to the starting chain in Round 1 and work one extra plain round in the sides section, the same principle used across our sock and slipper patterns: the chain length controls how long the sole is, while the side rounds control how deep it sits around the foot.

As a rough guide: Ch 9 (18 sts) suits 0–6 months, Ch 11 (22 sts) suits 6–12 months, and Ch 13 (26 sts) suits 12–18 months, adding one more side round at each step up. Always measure against the actual baby or a well-fitting existing shoe before starting if you can, since babies of the same age vary more in foot size than the "months" labels suggest.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the finished booties keep slipping off the baby's feet.
Fix: work the last round or two of the top opening with a smaller hook to snug it in, or add the optional ankle strap with a securely sewn button described above.
Mistake: the sole comes out narrow and pointed at the ends instead of a rounded oval.
Fix: double-check the stitch count after Round 1 (18 sts) and Round 2 (24 sts) — a miscounted increase round is almost always the cause, and it's easier to rip back two rounds than to keep going on a mis-shaped sole.
Mistake: the top opening is too tight to get over the foot, or too loose once it's on.
Fix: for a tight opening, gather the finishing round more loosely or add a stitch or two evenly around; for a loose one, gather it tighter or drop to a smaller hook for the last round before fastening off.

Frequently asked questions

What yarn is safe to use for crochet baby booties?

Soft, machine-washable acrylic or cotton yarn labelled for baby items is the standard choice. Avoid novelty or mohair-type yarns with loose fibres, which can be a safety concern for infants.

How do I size crochet baby booties for different ages?

This pattern fits 0-6 months. For a larger size, add one more increase round to the sole before starting the sides, which adds roughly a size.

Should baby booties have buttons or ties to stay on?

A simple ankle strap with a button works well for older babies who can't easily remove it, but for very young infants, snug-fitting booties without small attachable parts are the safer choice.

Why do my baby booties keep falling off?

The ankle opening is likely too loose relative to the leg — work the last round or two of the cuff with a smaller hook, or add a simple button or ribbon tie to hold them snug.

How do I wash handmade baby booties?

Hand wash in cool water with a mild, fragrance-free detergent and lay flat to dry; check the yarn label first, since some acrylic and cotton blends can also go on a gentle machine cycle inside a mesh bag.
