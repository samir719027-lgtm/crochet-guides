Corner to Corner (C2C) Crochet: Complete Beginner Tutorial

Pin this pattern

The stitch behind graphic crochet blankets, built diagonally in small blocks — easier than it looks.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 8 min read

Corner to corner, usually shortened to C2C, is the technique behind those graphic crochet blankets with pixel-art images or bold geometric patterns. It looks complex, but it's built from small blocks of double crochet worked diagonally — the same stitch from our granny square tutorial, just organised differently.

Quick answer: C2C works in diagonal rows of small 3-double-crochet "blocks." You increase blocks on one side to grow the piece into a triangle, then decrease on the other side to bring it back down into a square or rectangle.

What you need

  • A 5.0mm hook (see our hook buying guide if you don't have one)
  • Worsted-weight yarn in one or more colours
  • Scissors and a yarn needle for weaving in ends

Making your first block

Steps

  1. Chain 6.
  2. Double crochet in the 4th chain from the hook, then double crochet in each of the remaining 2 chains. This is your first block. (1 block)

Increasing: growing the triangle

Steps (repeat for each new row while increasing)

  1. Chain 6, turn.
  2. Double crochet in the 4th chain from hook, then in each of the remaining 2 chains — this makes your new block.
  3. Slip stitch into the first double crochet of the previous block to join, then chain 3 and double crochet 3 more times into the next chain-3 space of the previous row's block.
  4. Continue in this way, adding one block per row, until your piece is as wide as you want the finished project to be.

Decreasing: bringing it back to a square

Steps (once you've reached your desired width)

  1. Instead of chaining 6 to start a new block, slip stitch across the first block of the previous row to skip it entirely.
  2. Chain 3, then double crochet 3 times into the next chain-3 space to continue the row with one fewer block than before.
  3. Repeat, losing one block per row, until only a single block remains.

Changing colours for graphic designs

Once you're comfortable with the increase and decrease structure, changing yarn colour at the start of any block lets you build up pixel-style images or patterns, since each block reads as one "pixel" on a grid. Many free C2C graphs are written specifically for this technique, listing which colour each block should be.

If your edges look uneven: C2C tension can drift more than flat single crochet rows. Keep your chain-3 turning consistent and check block count against the row before, using our common mistakes guide if rows keep drifting in size.

Frequently asked questions

What is corner to corner (C2C) crochet?

C2C is a technique that builds a project diagonally from one corner to the opposite corner using small blocks of double crochet, commonly used for graphic blankets and pixel-style designs since each block can easily be a different colour.

Is C2C harder than regular crochet?

The stitches themselves are the same double crochet and chain used in many patterns; what's different is the counting and turning logic, which takes a bit of practice but isn't technically more advanced than basic crochet.

What is C2C crochet best used for?

C2C is especially popular for blankets and wall hangings with graphic or pixel-art designs, since each square block in the grid can be worked in a different colour to build up an image.
