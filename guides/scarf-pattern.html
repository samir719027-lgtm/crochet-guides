Free Single Crochet Scarf Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

The same skill as a dishcloth, just longer — a forgiving first wearable project.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 6in / 15cm wide, any length

I made three of these before I made anything else, and I mean that as encouragement rather than a confession. A long, one-row-repeat scarf is one of the few projects where doing it again immediately is actually the fastest way to improve, because there's nothing new to learn between attempts — just more reps of the same two stitches, which is exactly what makes your tension settle down. My first scarf started tight and finished loose. My third came out even from end to end, and that's really the whole point of this pattern.

This scarf is the longer cousin of our dishcloth pattern — same two stitches, same one repeating row, just kept going until it's scarf-length. It's a good next project once a dishcloth feels comfortable, and it's where most beginners notice their tension get more even simply from repetition.

Materials

  • 3–4 skeins worsted-weight yarn (about 600–700 yards total)
  • 5.0mm (US size H) crochet hook
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
chChain
scSingle crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)

New to abbreviations? Our abbreviations glossary covers US and UK terms side by side.

Gauge

14 sc and 16 rows measures about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.0mm hook and worsted yarn. Gauge isn't critical here — a scarf is forgiving on exact width.

Notes

  • Written in US terms. Check our abbreviations glossary first if you usually work in UK terms.
  • This pattern is just one row repeated — stop whenever your scarf reaches the length you want, roughly 60–70in / 150–180cm for a standard adult scarf.
  • If your yarn runs out mid-row, join a new skein at the start of the next row to keep the join tidy.

Pattern

Foundation

Chain 19.

Row 1

Single crochet in the 2nd chain from the hook, and in each chain across. Turn. (18 sts)

Row 2 and all following rows

Chain 1 (does not count as a stitch), single crochet in each stitch across. Turn. (18 sts)

Repeat this row until the scarf reaches your desired length. Check your stitch count every few rows — it should stay at 18 throughout.

Finishing

Fasten off, leaving a 6in / 15cm tail. Weave in all ends with a yarn needle, including any joins from changing skeins.

Customizing your scarf

Because this whole pattern is one repeating row, it's an easy one to make your own once the basic version feels comfortable. To change the width, chain a different odd starting number: chain 15 for a narrower scarf, or chain 23 for a wider one, then follow Row 1 and every following row exactly as written — the stitch count in the pattern will simply shift to match your new chain. For stripes, change colour at the start of any row by finishing the last stitch of the previous row with the new colour instead of the old one, so the join sits neatly at the edge rather than in the middle of a row.

If you'd rather work a slightly faster, softer fabric, half double crochet substitutes cleanly for single crochet throughout: chain the same number, work hdc instead of sc in every row, and expect the scarf to grow noticeably quicker with a bit more drape.

Common mistakes & fixes

Width drifts wider or narrower partway through. This usually means a stitch was missed or added at an edge, often from working into the turning chain by accident or skipping the very first stitch of a row. See our common mistakes guide for how to spot and fix it, and count your stitches every few rows so it's caught early.
Edges curl instead of lying flat. A small amount of curl is normal for single crochet and usually relaxes with blocking — wet the finished scarf, lay it flat to its intended dimensions, and let it air dry. If the curl is severe, your tension is likely tighter than the pattern expects; try going up half a hook size.
Tension gets tighter as the scarf gets longer. This is extremely common on long, repetitive projects and is almost always hand fatigue rather than a technique problem. Take a short break every few rows instead of working the whole scarf in one sitting, and your tension will stay far more even end to end.

Frequently asked questions

How much yarn do I need for a crochet scarf?

A standard adult scarf uses roughly 600 to 700 yards of worsted-weight yarn, which usually means three to four 100-gram skeins depending on the brand.

How long should a beginner crochet scarf be?

A comfortable adult scarf is typically 60 to 70 inches long, though this pattern can be stopped at any length since it's just repeating one row.

Why does my scarf get narrower or wider as I go?

This usually means a stitch is being added or dropped at the edge of a row, often from missing the first or last stitch, or from working an extra stitch into the turning chain. Count your stitches at the end of every row to catch it early.

How do I make this scarf wider or narrower?

Chain a different starting number, always in odd numbers to keep the same edge structure: chain 15 for a narrower scarf, or chain 23 for a wider one, then work Row 1 and all following rows exactly as written.

Why is my tension tighter at the end of a row than the start?

This is extremely common on long, repetitive rows and usually comes from your hand tiring, not a technique problem. Take a short break every few rows rather than pushing through an entire scarf in one sitting, and your tension will stay far more consistent.
