Easy Crochet Earrings Pattern (Mini Hoops)

Pin this pattern

Tiny hoops wrapped in single crochet — one of the fastest projects on this site, and a good way to use leftover yarn.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 15 min read

Each earring is made by single crocheting directly around a small hoop until it's fully covered, then attaching an earring hook — no separate stitching or shaping required.

Quick answer: Cover a small metal or plastic hoop completely in single crochet, then attach an earring hook to the top — repeat for a matching second earring.

What you need

  • A small amount of fine or sport weight yarn (or embroidery thread, for a more delicate look)
  • 2 small metal or plastic hoops, about 2–3cm
  • 2.5mm hook
  • Yarn needle, 2 earring hooks, small pliers

Step 1: cover the hoop

Steps

  1. Leaving a short tail, make a slip knot and place it on your hook.
  2. Insert your hook through the center of the hoop, yarn over, and pull up a loop, then chain 1 to anchor your yarn to the hoop.
  3. Work single crochet stitches around the hoop, inserting your hook around the hoop itself each time (not into a previous stitch), packing the stitches closely so no metal shows through.
  4. Continue around until the entire hoop is covered, then slip stitch into your first stitch to join.

Step 2: finish and attach

Steps

  1. Fasten off, leaving a short tail, and weave in both ends securely through the covered stitches, not through the hoop itself, so they don't show.
  2. Open an earring hook sideways with pliers (never pull it directly apart, which weakens the loop), slide it onto the top of your covered hoop, and close it again.
  3. Repeat everything for a second, matching earring.

A demonstration of small-scale crochet, useful for seeing how thread and a small hook behave differently from yarn.

Frequently asked questions

What yarn weight works best?

Fine, sport, or embroidery thread gives the neatest coverage; worsted weight will look bulky on small hoops.

Can I add beads?

Yes — thread beads onto your yarn before starting, then slide one into place every few stitches as you work around the hoop.

Do the two earrings need to match exactly?

For a polished look, use the same stitch count on both, though slightly mismatched handmade earrings are also a popular intentional style.
