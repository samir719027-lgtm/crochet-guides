Free Single Crochet Dishcloth Pattern for Beginners

Pin this pattern

A true beginner pattern using only chain and single crochet — finishes in under an hour.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 6–7 in / 15–18 cm square

My very first finished crochet project was a dishcloth, and I still think it's the right place for anyone to start — not because it's exciting, but because it's small enough that a mistake costs you twenty minutes instead of a whole weekend. I made four of them in a row before moving on to anything else, and by the fourth one my tension had gone from wildly inconsistent to something that actually looked intentional.

This is the same dishcloth from our 5 easiest first projects guide, written out in full so you can follow it row by row. It's built entirely from two stitches — chain and single crochet — and stays a flat, consistent square from start to finish, so mistakes are easy to spot and cheap to redo.

Materials

  • 1 skein worsted-weight cotton yarn (about 50–60 yards used)
  • 5.0mm (US size H) crochet hook
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
chChain
scSingle crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)

Gauge

Exact gauge isn't critical for this project — a dishcloth is forgiving on size. As a rough guide, 14 sc and 16 rows should measure about 4in / 10cm square with a 5.0mm hook and worsted yarn.

Notes

  • Written in US terms. If you usually work in UK terms, see our abbreviations glossary before starting.
  • The starting chain length below makes a roughly 7in / 18cm square. Chain a few stitches fewer or more for a smaller or larger cloth — just keep your starting chain an odd number.
  • Cotton yarn is recommended over acrylic for absorbency and durability through repeated washing.

Pattern

Foundation

Chain 21.

Row 1

Single crochet in the 2nd chain from the hook, and in each chain across. Turn. (20 sts)

Rows 2–26

Chain 1 (does not count as a stitch), single crochet in each stitch across. Turn. (20 sts)

Repeat this row until your piece is roughly square — about 26 rows for a 7in / 18cm cloth, checking against your starting chain edge as you go.

Finishing

Fasten off, leaving a 6in / 15cm tail. Weave in both ends with a yarn needle.

Resizing & variations

To make this dishcloth a different size, chain any odd number of stitches for the width, then repeat Row 2 until the piece measures roughly square against its own starting edge — there's no fixed row count to hit, just "keep going until it's a square." A smaller washcloth might only need 15-17 chains; an oversized one for bigger dishes could go up to 27 or 29.

Two easy additions once the basic square feels comfortable: a single round of sc worked evenly around all four edges gives a tidier finished look and a natural spot to add a hanging loop. Colour stripes just mean switching yarn colour at the start of any row — carry the unused colour up the side if you'll use it again within a few rows, or cut and rejoin if it'll be a while.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the edges curl instead of lying flat.
Fix: this is almost always tension tighter than the pattern's gauge. Try a 5.5mm hook next time, or block the finished piece flat while damp to help it hold its shape.
Mistake: the cloth comes out more rectangle than square.
Fix: row height and chain width don't always grow at the same rate. Rather than trusting a fixed row count, hold the working piece up against its own starting edge every few rows and stop once the two sides match, regardless of the exact row number.
Mistake: the finished dishcloth feels stiff or scratchy rather than soft.
Fix: this is usually the yarn, not the stitch pattern. Acrylic yarn tends to feel stiffer for kitchen use; a 100% cotton yarn softens noticeably after its first wash and holds up better to repeated scrubbing.

Frequently asked questions

How much yarn do I need for a crochet dishcloth?

One dishcloth uses roughly 50 to 60 yards of worsted-weight cotton yarn, so a single 100-gram skein is enough for two or three dishcloths.

Why is cotton yarn recommended for dishcloths instead of acrylic?

Cotton is more absorbent and holds up better to repeated washing and scrubbing than acrylic, which can pill and doesn't absorb water as well. It's the standard fibre choice for anything meant to get wet and washed often.

How do I keep the edges of my dishcloth from curling?

Curling on a single crochet dishcloth is usually caused by tension tighter than the pattern's gauge. Try a slightly larger hook, and block the finished piece flat while damp to help it hold its shape.

Can I make this dishcloth a different size?

Yes. Chain any odd number of stitches for the width you want, then work rows of single crochet until the piece is roughly square, checking the row height against the starting chain edge as you go rather than counting to a fixed row number.

Can I add a border or stripes to this dishcloth?

Yes, both are simple additions. A round of single crochet worked evenly around all four edges after finishing gives a tidier edge and a spot to add a hanging loop. Stripes just mean changing colour at the start of any row, carrying the unused colour up the side if you'll use it again soon.
