Crochet Mini Christmas Tree Pattern

Pin this pattern

A small stuffed cone-shaped tree, topped with a star and dotted with tiny bead ornaments.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 40 min read

This tree is a single stuffed cone worked in continuous rounds — similar shaping to the gnome body in our Christmas gnome pattern — finished standing upright with a flat base, a few stitched-on beads for ornaments, and a tiny star or pom-pom on top.

Quick answer: Crochet a wide flat base, then build a tall cone shape on top of it worked in continuous rounds without further increasing, stuff it firmly, and decorate with beads and a top star.

What you need

  • Worsted weight (#4) green yarn
  • 4.0mm (US G/6) hook
  • Polyester fiberfill, or dried rice/poly pellets for the base plus fiberfill above it
  • Small beads or seed beads in bright colors, for ornaments
  • A small amount of gold or yellow yarn for the star topper
  • Yarn needle

Base

Steps

  1. Magic ring, 6 sc into the ring. (6 sts)
  2. Round 2: 2 sc in each st around. (12 sts)
  3. Round 3: *sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (18 sts)
  4. Round 4: *2 sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (24 sts) — this becomes the flat base the tree stands on.

Cone body

Steps

  1. Without further increasing, work even in sc for 14–18 rounds, working in the back loop only for the first round after the base to create a defined edge where the cone rises.
  2. As you work, add a few tablespoons of dried rice or poly pellets in the very bottom of the base for stability, then continue stuffing firmly with fiberfill as the cone grows.
  3. For the final 4–5 rounds, decrease by 3–6 sts per round to close the cone to a small point at the top, stuffing right up until it closes.
  4. Fasten off, leaving a small tail at the top for attaching the star.

Decorating

Steps

  1. Star topper: work a tiny 5-point star from gold yarn (chain a small star shape, or use a small crocheted circle as a simpler stand-in), and stitch it to the point of the tree.
  2. Sew small beads scattered over the surface of the cone to look like ornaments.
  3. Optional: add a thin strand of yarn spiraled loosely around the cone and tacked in a few places to look like a garland.

Frequently asked questions

How do I keep the tree standing upright?

Weight the very bottom of the base with a little dried rice or poly pellets sealed in a small bag before adding regular fiberfill above it.

Can I make a whole forest of different sizes?

Yes — use a smaller base and fewer rounds for mini trees, or a larger base and more rounds for a taller centerpiece tree.

What if I don't want to make a star topper?

A simple pom-pom, a small bead, or even a tiny bow works just as well on top.
