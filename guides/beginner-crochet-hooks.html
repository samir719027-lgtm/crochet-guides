If you've just started looking into crochet, the hook aisle is overwhelming. Dozens of sizes, three or four materials, ergonomic handles, bamboo, steel — and every listing claims to be "best for beginners." It isn't. Here's what actually matters for your first hook, and which type fits your hands and your budget.

Quick answer: if you only buy one hook today, get a 5.0mm (US size H) aluminum hook with a soft handle. It's the right size for common yarn, easy to see your stitches on, and comfortable enough to practice with for 30+ minutes without hand fatigue.

What actually matters in a first hook

Three things decide whether your first hook helps you learn or makes you quit in week one:

  • Size. Too small and you can't see your stitches. Too large and tension gets sloppy. 5.0mm is the sweet spot for worsted-weight yarn, the most common beginner yarn.
  • Handle. A bare metal shaft digs into your fingers after 15–20 minutes. A wider, soft-grip handle is the single upgrade that keeps beginners crocheting longer.
  • Hook shape. Inline hooks (the head is flush with the shaft) give more consistent tension for new crocheters than tapered hooks, which are easier to slip off mid-stitch.

Frequently asked questions

What size crochet hook should a beginner start with?

A 5.0mm (US size H) hook is the easiest starting point. It works with common worsted-weight yarn and is large enough to see your stitches clearly while you're still learning tension.

Are ergonomic crochet hooks worth it for beginners?

Yes, especially if you plan to crochet for more than 20 minutes at a time. The soft, wider handle reduces hand fatigue and cramping, which is the main reason new crocheters put the craft down early on.

Should I buy a full crochet hook set or a single hook?

A single 5.0mm hook is enough to learn the basics. A full set only pays off once you start following patterns that call for specific sizes, usually after your first few projects.
