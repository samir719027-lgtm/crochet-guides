What actually matters in a first hook

Three things decide whether your first hook helps you learn or makes you quit in week one:

  • Size. Too small and you can't see your stitches. Too large and tension gets sloppy. 5.0mm is the sweet spot for worsted-weight yarn, the most common beginner yarn.
  • Handle. A bare metal shaft digs into your fingers after 15–20 minutes. A wider, soft-grip handle is the single upgrade that keeps beginners crocheting longer.
  • Hook shape. Inline hooks (the head is flush with the shaft) give more consistent tension for new crocheters than tapered hooks, which are easier to slip off mid-stitch.

Grip & hand comfort

Beyond size and material, how you naturally hold a hook affects which handle shape actually feels comfortable. A pencil grip — holding the hook like a pen, between thumb and forefinger — tends to pair well with a slimmer handle, since there's less bulk to work around. A knife grip — holding it overhand, the way you'd hold a table knife — often benefits more from a wider, ergonomic soft-grip handle, since more of the hand is in contact with the shaft.

Neither grip is more correct, and most crocheters land on one without ever consciously choosing it. If your hand cramps within the first 10-15 minutes, it's worth trying the other grip on the same hook before assuming you need different equipment entirely.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: buying a hook too small to comfortably see your stitches.
Fix: stick to 5.0mm for your very first hook regardless of what a pattern eventually calls for. It's large enough to see clearly while learning tension, and common enough that you won't struggle to find matching yarn.
Mistake: quitting early because of hand cramping, assuming it's a personal limitation.
Fix: this is very often the hook, not the hand. A bare metal shaft with no handle is the single most common cause of early hand fatigue. Switching to a soft-grip ergonomic handle resolves it for most people within one project.
Mistake: accidentally buying a steel hook meant for thread crochet.
Fix: steel hooks use a separate, much smaller sizing system meant for lace and thread work, not standard yarn. Check that a hook is labelled in millimetres somewhere in the 2-10mm range before buying it for a yarn project.

Frequently asked questions

What size crochet hook should a beginner start with?

A 5.0mm (US size H) hook is the easiest starting point. It works with common worsted-weight yarn and is large enough to see your stitches clearly while you're still learning tension.

Are ergonomic crochet hooks worth it for beginners?

Yes, especially if you plan to crochet for more than 20 minutes at a time. The soft, wider handle reduces hand fatigue and cramping, which is the main reason new crocheters put the craft down early on.

Should I buy a full crochet hook set or a single hook?

A single 5.0mm hook is enough to learn the basics. A full set only pays off once you start following patterns that call for specific sizes, usually after your first few projects.

Does how I hold my hook affect which one I should buy?

Somewhat. A pencil grip, holding the hook like a pen, tends to pair well with a slimmer handle, while a knife grip, holding it overhand like a knife, often feels more comfortable with a wider ergonomic handle. Neither grip is more correct; try both on a cheap hook before investing in a full set.

Why did I accidentally buy a hook that's too thin for my yarn?

This usually happens with steel hooks, which are sized for thread crochet and lace work, not standard yarn, and use a different, much smaller numbering system than regular yarn hooks. Check that a hook is labelled in millimetres in the 2-10mm range before buying for a yarn project.
