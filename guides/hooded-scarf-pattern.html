Crochet Hooded Scarf Pattern: Easy Hooded Cowl Step-by-Step

Pin this guide

Combine a warm cowl with an integrated hood using straightforward double crochet stitches and soft Aran yarn.

Updated 2026 · 8 min read

A hooded scarf—sometimes called a scoodie or hooded cowl—is one of the most practical winter accessories you can make. It protects your neck and head from cold drafts without slipping off like a loose beanie or dangling scarf ends.

In this tutorial, we construct the hooded scarf as a seamless project worked from the cowl base upward into the hood. Using basic double crochet stitches in medium weight yarn, you will get a cozy, textured piece that comes together surprisingly quickly.

Quick answer: To crochet a hooded scarf, start by working a simple cowl foundation in the round, then transition to flat rows across the back to form the hood shell. Seam the top edge folded flat, and finish with a decorative border around the face opening.

Materials and Sizing Basics

To make a standard adult hooded scarf, you will need approximately 550 to 650 yards of worsted (Category 4) or Aran weight yarn and a 5.5mm (I-9) crochet hook. Acrylic or wool-blend yarns work best because they offer flexibility and heat retention without becoming too heavy on your neck.

Besides yarn and your hook, keep a tapestry needle ready for joining the top of the hood and weaving in ends. Two stitch markers will help you position the hood opening accurately on top of the cowl.

  • 550-650 yards worsted weight yarn
  • 5.5mm (US I-9) crochet hook
  • Tapestry needle and stitch markers

Step 1: Working the Cowl Foundation

Chain 72 stitches and join with a slip stitch to form a large ring, making sure your chain is not twisted. Row 1: Chain 2 (does not count as a stitch), work 1 double crochet into each chain around, and join to the first double crochet.

Continue working double crochet in the round for 12 rounds total. This creates a comfortable 7-inch cowl neck that sits neatly around your collar without bunching up under a winter coat.

Step 2: Shaping the Hood

Now you will stop working in the round and turn to work flat rows across two-thirds of the cowl circumference. Skip 24 stitches in the front for your neck gap, attach your yarn to the next stitch, and double crochet across the remaining 48 stitches.

Chain 2 and turn at the end of each row. Continue working flat back-and-forth rows across these 48 stitches for 22 rows, or until the hood measures approximately 12 inches from the cowl join to the top edge.

Step 3: Seaming and Finishing Touches

Fold the top edge of the hood in half with right sides together. Use your tapestry needle and yarn to seam the top edge together using a whip stitch or mattress stitch, creating the top crown of your hood.

Turn the hooded scarf right side out. Reattach yarn at the bottom front join and work two rounds of single crochet around the entire face opening and cowl edge to create a neat, stable trim that prevents stretching.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to crochet a hooded scarf?

For a beginner using worsted yarn, a hooded scarf typically takes between 6 and 9 hours of active stitching time.

Can I make this pattern with chunky yarn?

Yes, but you will need to reduce your initial chain count and total row count to match your gauge so the hood does not end up oversized.

Is a hooded scarf suitable for absolute beginners?

Yes. If you know how to chain, double crochet, and work basic seaming, this project is very manageable.

How do I make the hood deeper for bulky hair?

Add 2 to 4 extra rows to the flat hood portion before seaming the top edge.

What is the best stitch for a warm hooded scarf?

Double crochet creates a balanced stitch height with good drape, though half double crochet yields an even denser, warmer fabric.
