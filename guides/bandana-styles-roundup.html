10 Crochet Bandana Styles You Can Try

Pin this pattern

From classic granny triangles to lace and appliqué designs — ten directions to take your next bandana project.

Updated 2026 · 6 min read

Once you've made one bandana, it's easy to see why so many crocheters keep coming back to the shape — small, fast, and endlessly adaptable. Here are ten different directions other pattern designers have taken it, from beginner-friendly basics to a few that stretch your stitch vocabulary a little further.

01

Classic granny triangle

The most common starting point: a plain granny stitch triangle worked top-down, finished with simple ties. A textbook beginner project that delivers a clean, polished result quickly.

See the pattern at The Funky Stitch →
02

Diamond lace bandana

A more advanced take using a diamond lace stitch and optional picot edging. The designer includes a pixel grid to help with row counting, which makes the stitch pattern easier to follow than it looks.

See the pattern at VivCrochets →
03

Granny square patchwork

Instead of one continuous triangle, this style joins several granny squares and half-squares together, so each colour block can be a different shade for a patchwork look.

See the pattern at Crocht →
04

Floral appliqué bandana

A plain triangle base with a separately crocheted flower or leaf motif sewn on afterward — a good way to practise small appliqué pieces without committing to a whole floral design.

See the pattern at Crocht →
05

Watermelon bandana

A playful colourwork version using pink, green, and black sections to suggest a watermelon slice. A fun way to practise colour changes on a small, low-commitment project.

See the pattern at Smart Knit Crocheting →
06

Ombre bandana

Worked in a single colour family that gradually shifts from light to dark across the triangle, using graduated skeins or hand-changing yarn every few rows.

See the pattern at Pattern Center →
07

Bandana cowl

A hybrid piece that combines the look of a bandana with the closed loop of a cowl, so it slips over the head rather than tying — useful for anyone who finds knots fiddly.

See the pattern at DIY Make To →
08

Boho headscarf with built-in ties

A romantic, fingering-weight version designed specifically to sit low across the forehead, with ties long enough to knot at the nape for a relaxed, festival-style look.

See the pattern at Joy of Motion Crochet →
09

Baby-to-adult sized set

A lacy pattern graded across five sizes, from infant to adult, so the whole family can wear a matching version — a nice project if you're making several at once.

See the pattern at Joy of Motion Crochet →
10

Our own beginner-friendly version

If most of the styles above feel like a stretch right now, our own bandana pattern sticks to plain double crochet throughout — no lace repeats or colour changes to track, just a straightforward triangle and two ties.
