Crochet Christmas Coasters Pattern

Pin this pattern

A set of four round coasters in holiday colors, each topped with a simple embroidered snowflake.

Updated 2026 · US terms · 35 min read

Each coaster is a simple flat circle worked in the round — the same basic increases used in our everyday coasters pattern — with a tiny embroidered snowflake added on top for a seasonal touch. A great way to use up small amounts of holiday-colored scrap yarn.

Quick answer: Crochet a flat circle in the round using standard 6-stitch-per-round increases until it reaches about 4 inches across, then embroider a simple cross-stitch snowflake on top. Repeat 4 times.

What you need

  • Worsted weight (#4) cotton yarn, small amounts in red, green, and cream
  • 4.0mm (US G/6) hook
  • Tapestry needle for the embroidered snowflake
  • Contrast color thread or yarn for the snowflake detail

Coaster body (make 4)

Steps

  1. Magic ring, 6 sc into the ring. (6 sts)
  2. Round 2: 2 sc in each st around. (12 sts)
  3. Round 3: *sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (18 sts)
  4. Round 4: *2 sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (24 sts)
  5. Round 5: *3 sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (30 sts)
  6. Round 6: *4 sc, 2 sc in next st* around. (36 sts) — this should measure roughly 4 inches across; add another increase round for a larger coaster.
  7. Slip stitch to close, fasten off, weave in the end.

Snowflake detail

Steps

  1. Thread contrast yarn or embroidery thread onto a tapestry needle.
  2. Working from the center of the coaster, stitch 3 straight lines crossing at the middle (6 points total), each about 1 inch long.
  3. Add tiny diagonal stitches at the tips of each line for a simple snowflake look.
  4. Weave in the ends on the back of the coaster.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make these without embroidery?

Yes — plain circles in holiday colors still work well on their own, or swap the snowflake for a contrast-color edging round instead.

What yarn gives the best coasters?

A cotton or cotton-blend yarn holds its shape and absorbs condensation better than acrylic, which is worth it for anything that will sit under a mug or glass.

How do I make them lie flatter?

Steam-block the finished circles under a damp cloth and let them dry flat under light weight, which helps flatten any natural cupping from the increases.
