Free Crochet Amigurumi Bear Pattern for Beginners

A simple stuffed bear built from the same shaping as our mini ball pattern, with four limbs and rounded ears added on.

Updated 2026 · US terms · About 6in / 15cm tall

The first bear I ever made went to my niece, and I sewed the ears on without stepping back to check them first — one sat noticeably higher than the other, giving the poor thing a permanently startled expression. She loved him anyway and named him Wonky, which is either a lesson in not worrying too much or a reminder to lay pieces out before sewing. I still do the second one now.

This bear scales up the same increase-and-decrease shaping from our mini amigurumi ball pattern into a full body, head, and four limbs. It's a bigger project than our other amigurumi patterns, but every individual piece uses the same simple technique repeated.

Toy safety note: for children under 3, embroider the face with yarn instead of using plastic safety eyes. Stitch all seams tightly and check periodically for wear. See the FAQ below for more detail.

Materials

  • 1–2 skeins worsted-weight yarn (about 80–100 yards)
  • 3.5mm hook (smaller than standard, for tight amigurumi stitches)
  • Polyester fiberfill stuffing
  • Safety eyes, or embroidery thread for a child-safe embroidered face
  • Stitch marker, to track the start of each round
  • Scissors and a yarn needle

Gauge

Gauge isn't critical — the goal is tight, even stitches that hide the stuffing. If you can see stuffing through your stitches, try a smaller hook.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, worked in a continuous spiral, same technique as our mini amigurumi ball pattern.
  • Made from five separate pieces (head, body, 2 arms, 2 legs, 2 ears) sewn together at the end.
  • For a child under 3, skip plastic safety eyes and embroider the face instead.

Pattern

Head, Rounds 1–3

Magic ring, 6 sc. (6) → Inc around. (12) → [sc 1, inc] around. (18)

Head, Rounds 4–8

Sc every st around for 5 rounds, no shaping. (18 sts each round)

Head, Rounds 9–10

[sc 1, dec] around. (12) → Dec around, stuffing firmly as you close. (6)

Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing to the body.

Body, Rounds 1–3

Magic ring, 6 sc. (6) → Inc around. (12) → [sc 1, inc] around. (18)

Body, Rounds 4–7

Sc every st around for 4 rounds. (18 sts each round)

Body, Rounds 8–9

[sc 1, dec] around. (12) → Dec around, stuffing as you close. (6)

Fasten off, leaving a long tail for sewing to the head.

Arms (make 2)

Magic ring, 5 sc. (5). Sc every st around for 5 rounds, no shaping, stuffing lightly. Fasten off, leaving a tail for sewing.

Legs (make 2)

Magic ring, 6 sc. (6). Sc every st around for 4 rounds, stuffing lightly. Fasten off, leaving a tail for sewing.

Ears (make 2)

Magic ring, 5 sc into ring. Fasten off, leaving a short tail for sewing on flat, without stuffing.

Assembly

Sew the body to the bottom of the head using the tails from each. Sew the two ears onto the top of the head, and the two arms and two legs onto the body, checking placement is even before securing each with small, tight stitches. Attach safety eyes before stuffing the head if using them, or embroider a simple face with yarn once assembly is complete for a child-safe alternative.

Giving it personality

Small placement choices change the bear's expression more than the stitch pattern does. Setting the safety eyes slightly closer together than feels natural reads as younger and more endearing; spacing them wider gives a more alert, wide-eyed look. Angling the ears slightly forward rather than straight up also softens the face, and a few embroidered stitches for eyebrows above the eyes can add surprising character for very little extra work.

A small accessory is an easy way to make a plain bear feel finished: a scrap-yarn chain tied as a bow around the neck, a few single crochet stitches worked as a tiny scarf, or a contrast-colour patch of single crochet sewn on as a belly panel all take under ten minutes and make the bear feel like a deliberate design rather than a basic shape.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the ears, arms, or legs end up sewn on unevenly, giving the bear a lopsided look.
Fix: lay all pieces out on a flat surface before sewing anything, checking placement from directly above, and pin or mark the spot with a scrap of yarn before stitching each one in place.
Mistake: safety eyes end up spaced too far apart or too close together once inserted.
Fix: insert the eye posts but don't lock the backings on yet — hold the head up and check the spacing looks right from a normal viewing distance before securing them permanently.
Mistake: one body part looks noticeably larger or smaller than the others once everything is assembled.
Fix: stuff each piece to a similar firmness as you close it, and compare pieces side by side before sewing — a piece that looks slightly small can often be evened out with a bit more stuffing pushed in through the opening before it's fully closed.

Frequently asked questions

Is this amigurumi bear pattern safe for young children?

For children under 3, embroider the facial features with yarn instead of using plastic safety eyes, since even securely attached safety eyes carry some small-parts risk for very young children.

How long does an amigurumi bear take to make?

Including the head, body, and four limbs, most beginners finish this bear over two to three sessions, roughly 3 to 4 hours of total crochet time plus assembly.

How do I attach the limbs to an amigurumi body?

Sew each limb on with the tail left from finishing it, using a yarn needle and small, tight stitches around the entire attachment point rather than just a few anchor points.

Can I wash a finished amigurumi bear?

Spot clean with a damp cloth and mild soap where possible; a full wash risks matting the stuffing and loosening safety eyes, so it's best avoided unless the bear is machine-washable by design.

What's the best stuffing for an amigurumi bear?

Polyester fiberfill is the standard choice because it stays soft, holds its shape, and is washable and hypoallergenic, unlike natural fibers that can clump or felt over time.
