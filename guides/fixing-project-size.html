How to Fix a Crochet Project That's Too Small or Too Big

Pin this pattern

Why your finished piece doesn't match the pattern's stated size, and what to actually check before your next attempt.

Updated 2026 · 5 min read

A finished project that comes out noticeably smaller or larger than the pattern promised is almost never bad luck — it's gauge. Once you know what to check, sizing problems become predictable and fixable, instead of a mystery that seems to happen at random.

Quick answer: too small usually means your tension is tighter than the pattern's gauge, or you're using a hook one size too small. Too big is the reverse. A quick gauge swatch before starting catches both before they become a finished, wrong-sized project.

Why it happens

Too small

Most likely cause: your natural tension is tighter than the designer's, or your hook is smaller than the pattern calls for. Both pack more stitches into the same space, shrinking the finished piece.

Fix for next time: go up half a hook size and re-check your gauge swatch before starting the actual project.

Too big

Most likely cause: the reverse — looser tension than the designer's, or a hook one size larger than recommended.

Fix for next time: drop half a hook size, and check whether your stitches are loose enough to see gaps between them, which is often a visible sign before you even measure.

How to actually check gauge before you start

  1. Crochet a swatch using the hook and yarn weight the pattern specifies, at least 4 inches (10cm) square.
  2. Lay it flat without stretching it, and measure how many stitches and rows fit into that 4-inch square.
  3. Compare your count to the pattern's stated gauge. If you have more stitches than the pattern expects, size up your hook. Fewer, size down.

If gauge terminology is still unfamiliar, our guide to reading a crochet pattern covers exactly what it means and how it's written into a pattern.

Can you fix a project that's already finished?

It depends on how far off it is. A small mismatch can sometimes be corrected with blocking — dampening the finished piece and reshaping it while it dries, which works especially well on natural fibres like cotton or wool. A significant size difference usually can't be blocked away, and the more reliable fix is unravelling and reworking with an adjusted hook, using the checks above before you start again.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my finished crochet project come out smaller than the pattern says?

The most common cause is tighter tension than the pattern's gauge, or using a hook one size smaller than recommended. Both result in more stitches per inch than the pattern expects, shrinking the final size.

Can I fix a crochet project after it's already finished?

For a small size difference, blocking (dampening and reshaping the fabric while it dries) can adjust dimensions slightly, especially with natural fibres. For a significant size mismatch, the fabric usually needs to be unravelled and reworked with an adjusted hook size or stitch count.

Do I need to check gauge for every crochet project?

For fitted items like garments or amigurumi with matching parts, yes. For forgiving projects like blankets or scarves where an inch either way doesn't matter, most crocheters skip the swatch and adjust as they go.
