Materials

  • Small amount of worsted-weight cotton yarn (under 20 yards per bookmark)
  • 4.5mm (US size 7) crochet hook
  • Scissors
  • Yarn needle, for weaving in ends and adding an optional tassel

Abbreviations (US terms)

Abbr.Means
chChain
scSingle crochet
st(s)Stitch(es)

Gauge

Gauge isn't critical for this small project. Any consistent worsted-weight tension will produce a usable bookmark around 1.5in / 4cm wide.

Notes

  • Written in US terms, using the same flat single crochet technique as our dishcloth pattern.
  • Makes one bookmark; repeat 2 more times for a set of three.
  • A great project for small leftover amounts of yarn from other projects, like the bits left over from our coasters pattern.

Pattern (make 3)

Foundation

Chain 5.

Row 1

Single crochet in the 2nd chain from the hook, and in each chain across. Turn. (4 sts)

Rows 2–24

Chain 1 (does not count as a stitch), single crochet in each stitch across. Turn. (4 sts each row)

Repeat this row until the strip measures about 6in / 15cm, roughly 24 rows for worsted-weight yarn.

Finishing

Fasten off, leaving a 6in / 15cm tail. Weave in the starting end with a yarn needle. Use the remaining tail to add an optional tassel, or weave it in too for a plain finished edge.

A step-by-step demonstration of crocheting a simple bookmark, useful for seeing the stitch count and finishing tassel technique.

Adding a tassel: cut 6–8 strands of yarn about 4in / 10cm long, fold in half, and loop through the bottom edge of the bookmark. Tie tightly at the fold and trim the ends even.

Strip vs corner bookmark

A flat strip is the simplest bookmark, but it's not the only shape worth knowing. A corner bookmark is a small folded triangle that slips over the corner of a page, and it holds its place a little more securely than a strip that can slide out of a paperback. The strip you're making here wins on speed and elegance — it lies flat, fits any book, and finishes in about twenty minutes — while a corner version needs shaping and a small seam. I tell beginners to make a handful of strips first to build even tension, then try a corner bookmark once the single crochet rows feel automatic. Both start from the same single crochet foundation, so the skills transfer cleanly. If you want to try the corner shape, look for a folding triangle pattern once these strips feel easy — the difference is mostly in the final fold and seam.

Yarn, colour, and finish

Cotton is the right fibre here for a reason that has nothing to do with looks: it's smooth, so it glides between pages without snagging the way a fuzzy or textured yarn catches on paper. A worsted-weight mercerised cotton gives the crispest edge, and a solid, light colour shows the stitch definition best. For gifts, a deep jewel tone or a soft neutral reads more considered than a novelty multicolour, and a small tassel in the same or a contrast colour finishes the strip neatly. If your hands lean toward a tighter tension, our hook size chart can help you go up a size so the strip doesn't come out stiff.

A matching set for gifting

Three bookmarks tied with a length of ribbon make a gift far nicer than the sum of their parts, and because they need no sizing, they're a safe make for someone you don't know well. I like to make a set in three shades of the same colour — light, mid, and dark — or a matching trio of tassels. Work all three before you finish the ends so the lengths match, and lay them side by side as you go. Add a small note explaining they're cotton and can be hand-washed if they ever get grubby, and the gift is complete. It's the kind of present that costs almost nothing but feels personal in a way a shop card never does. Make the three strips in one sitting while the rhythm is fresh, and they'll match more closely than if you spread them across a week.

Common mistakes & fixes

Mistake: the strip curls into a tube instead of lying flat.
Fix: the starting chain was pulled too tight. Make the foundation chain loosely, or go up a hook size for the chain row only, then work the rest at your normal tension.
Mistake: the edges look wavy and uneven.
Fix: the stitch count drifted between rows. Count to four stitches on every row — losing or adding one at the edge is what makes the sides wander in and out.
Mistake: the bookmark is too wide for a paperback.
Fix: the starting chain was too long. Four stitches gives about 1.5 inches, which is right for most books — if you want it narrower, chain fewer stitches and keep that count on every row.
Mistake: the tassel pulls out of the bottom edge.
Fix: it was tied too loosely. Loop the folded strands through the edge, pull the ends through firmly, and cinch the knot tight before trimming — a loose tassel will slip out the first time it's tugged.
Mistake: a woven-in end works its way free over time.
Fix: the tail wasn't long enough to weave securely. Leave at least six inches and run it through four or five stitches before trimming, so it can't pop loose with handling.
Mistake: the yarn catches on book pages as you use it.
Fix: the yarn is too fuzzy or textured. Use a smooth cotton or a tightly spun yarn — fluffy or novelty yarns grab paper and pull the bookmark out of place.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a crochet bookmark take to make?

A single bookmark takes about 15 to 20 minutes once you're comfortable with basic single crochet, making it one of the fastest projects to finish in one sitting.

What yarn is best for a crochet bookmark?

A smooth worsted-weight cotton yarn works well, since it holds a crisp shape and won't snag on book pages the way a fuzzy or textured yarn might.

Can I add a tassel to a crochet bookmark?

Yes, a small tassel made from a few wrapped strands of yarn, tied off and trimmed, is a common and easy addition to the bottom of the bookmark for extra detail.

Can I make a crochet bookmark wider or longer?

Yes — add or remove stitches from the starting chain to change the width, and work more or fewer rows to change the length. Just keep the stitch count consistent across every row so the edges stay even.

How do I stop a crochet bookmark from curling?

Curling is usually a tension issue, most often from a starting chain pulled too tight or a foundation row worked at a different tightness than the rest. Blocking the finished strip with a little steam or a light damp press usually flattens it out.

How many bookmarks can I make from one ball of yarn?

A standard 50g ball of worsted-weight cotton makes far more than three — each bookmark uses under 20 yards, so a single ball can easily produce a dozen or more, making it one of the most economical projects on the site.
